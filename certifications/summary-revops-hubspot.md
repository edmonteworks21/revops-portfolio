# HubSpot Revenue Operations Certification, Notes & Summary

A working summary of the HubSpot Academy Revenue Operations (RevOps) Certification,
written in my own words as I completed the course. It captures the core concepts,
frameworks, and tools covered across all ten modules.

---

## What RevOps is

Revenue Operations unifies how a business generates revenue by aligning marketing,
sales, and customer success, rather than running them as separate silos. It uses the
data that every customer interaction creates to find and fix inefficiencies, so the
company can grow without simply adding headcount and cost.

The recurring thread across the whole certification: **process first, then data
governance, then tools, in that order.** Tools accelerate a process, so an undefined
process just gets amplified.

---

## Scale and the flywheel

- **Scale** = revenue grows faster than the cost required to generate it. Growth adds
  cost in step with revenue; scale does not. High-touch, manual service does not scale,
  so the goal is repeatable processes that preserve a good customer experience.
- **The flywheel** replaces the funnel. A funnel ends; in recurring-revenue models the
  customer is not an endpoint. The flywheel puts the customer at the center: attract,
  engage, delight, which feeds word of mouth and creates new prospects.
- **Force** = anything that speeds the flywheel up. **Friction** = anything that slows
  it down.
- Prioritize removing friction over adding force. But not all friction is bad, some is
  a useful tool (for example, requiring training before onboarding). Match the amount of
  friction to the value on the other side.

## Aligning sales and marketing: the SLA

An SLA is a two-way agreement: marketing commits to delivering qualified leads, sales
commits to working them (often within a set time, such as 24 hours).

- To size a basic SLA you need three numbers: lead-to-opportunity conversion rate,
  opportunity-to-sale conversion rate, and average sale value.
- Refine over time from lead **volume** to lead **value**, since not all MQLs are equal
  (an ebook download is not a demo request).
- Cadence matters too: delivering 100 leads in one week can overwhelm sales. Faster
  contact raises conversion, but message quality still matters.
- A basic SLA beats no SLA. Optimize it over time.

## Mapping the sales process

- Build the process around the **buyer's journey** (awareness, consideration, decision),
  not around your own offerings.
- Sales-process steps are the points where a buyer cannot move forward without a rep.
- Each step needs **exit criteria**: what moves the prospect forward and what signals
  the step is complete. Write steps buyer-centric ("product was evaluated") rather than
  seller-centric ("I gave a demo").
- A **methodology** tells reps what to do at each step; a **playbook** holds what to
  know, do, say, and show. Teams need a content framework, not just more content.
- Evaluate continuously. A process is either humming (80%+ of reps hitting quota),
  experimenting, or thrashing (jumping between fixes, which is corrosive).

## Systems and data

- Invest in three areas for good data: **process definition, data governance, and tech
  stack management.**
- **Data governance** manages the availability, usability, integrity, and security of
  data, and controls who can access it.
- A **data dictionary** documents every important field, its name in each system, how
  fields integrate, the direction data flows, and the **system of record** (the one tool
  that owns the truth for a given field).
- Keep it simple. You cannot add simplicity, you can only remove complexity. Evaluate
  every customization: is the value worth the added complexity?
- On tools: your job is not to keep the stack small, it is to have a say in what gets
  added and why. The tool matters less than the data it supports.

## Communicating value to leaders

Operations is often seen as an expense. The job is to translate cost into revenue
impact, in the language leadership speaks: dollars and cents.

- **Business finance basics:** the balance sheet (a point-in-time snapshot, Assets =
  Liabilities + Equity), the income statement (performance over a period: Revenue minus
  COGS = Gross profit, minus operating expenses = Operating income), and the cash flow
  statement (net money in and out).
- **Cost of Poor Quality (CoPQ):** a Lean Six Sigma tool that quantifies what poor
  quality costs, across internal failures, external failures, appraisal, and prevention.
  Used to make the financial case for investing in prevention.
- **The A3:** forces an idea onto one page. Left side is why it matters (background,
  current condition, goal, analysis); right side is the solution (countermeasure, plan,
  follow-up). A tool for developing thinking, not just documenting a decision.
- Supporting tools: **5 Whys** (ask why ~5 times to reach root cause), the **fishbone
  diagram** (map causes across people, methods, materials, equipment, environment,
  management), and **DARCI** (Decision maker, Accountable, Responsible, Consulted,
  Informed).
- **Monroe's Motivated Sequence** for persuasion: Attention, Need, Satisfaction,
  Visualization, Action. **Pre-wiring** (meeting stakeholders one-on-one before the
  group) builds advocates in advance.

## Structuring and hiring the team

- **Silos** are the main blocker to a good customer experience. Alignment matters more
  than strategy: differing strategies across teams create conflict.
- Favor a **customer-in** strategy (build around the customer experience) over
  **function-out** (build around what is easiest for each siloed team).
- Three candidate types: **experienced leaders**, **transitioning generalists** (broad
  operational skills coming into RevOps from an adjacent field), and **credentialed
  specialists**. A healthy team mixes all three.
- Hire for complementary strengths: assess your own gaps honestly first. Interviews
  should be standardized to reduce bias, prioritize **transferable skills** over direct
  ops experience, and look for **problem-solving shown through self-initiated projects.**

---

## The RevOps loop

The entire practice is one repeating cycle:

1. **Data** — implement systems that identify opportunities for growth.
2. **Communication** — communicate those opportunities to leaders and commit to a plan.
3. **Alignment** — align the relevant teams around the metrics and goals that support it.
4. **Measure and iterate** — track progress against short- and long-term goals, update
   the plan, then repeat as new opportunities and challenges emerge.

Turn opportunities into quantifiable goals, ladder short-term goals up to a long-term
vision, and break those down to daily priorities. A **stop-doing list** protects focus.
Don't overthink the start: begin, measure, iterate.
